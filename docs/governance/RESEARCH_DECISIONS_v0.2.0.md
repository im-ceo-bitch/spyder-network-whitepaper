# Spyder Network — research decision register (v0.2.0)

## Agreed brand names
Spyder Network / SpyderChain / SpyderCoin (SPD) / Spyder Wallet. The word Spyder is retained at the founder's request. Names are not trademark-registered or proven free to use internationally.

## Policy intent (agreed)
Build real user adoption and an economically sustainable network across 5–6 years. Do not promise a Bitcoin-like price trajectory. Do not spend on trademarks at this time. Publish a verifiable research Whitepaper before any token sale.

## Candidate parameters (NOT agreed or implemented)
- M-01 capped monetary supply of 9,999,999,999 SPD.
- Genesis mint 75% (locked allocation); future bounded validator rewards 25% over 15 years.
- Proposed allocation shares and vesting per Whitepaper section 6.
- Proposed fee split at mainnet: 90% validators, 10% protocol treasury, initially no burn.
- Hypothetical gas charge, validator costs and transaction volumes are illustrative stress-test inputs, not market measurements.

## Alternatives examined
- 100% minted at genesis: transparent cap but long-term security funding is precarious.
- Unbounded disinflationary PoS issuance: helps ongoing validator funding but is continuously dilutive.
- High burn or guaranteed staking APY: rejected as premature and potentially misleading.
- Mandatory SPD payments for every stablecoin transfer: rejected as unnecessary friction; sponsored gas may be appropriate if secured.

## Required technical decisions before v1.0
- Confirm actual code, consensus, independent validator permissioning, staking/slashing, finality, execution model, chain IDs and genesis.
- Determine launch year, unlock schedule by height/epoch, precision and rounding, treasury governance and multisig signers.
- Verify max supply enforcement, attacks on consensus/economic design, actual validator operation costs and fee-market behavior.
- Examine self-funding after year 15, e.g. fee-only security viability in low-volume scenarios; do not silently add inflation.
- Confirm public licence, actual author identity, names/ticker collision risk, regulatory limits on services and token sales.
